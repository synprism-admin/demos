Building a Strong Case Starts Here

When building your personal injury case, the evidence and documentation you gather can make a significant difference in the outcome. Our attorneys have compiled this guide to help you understand what to collect, what mistakes to avoid, and how to protect your rights from day one.

Don't worry if you don't have everything - we can help you identify and gather what's needed. The most important first step is to contact an attorney as soon as possible.

Important: California Statute of Limitations

In most personal injury cases in California, you have 2 years from the date of injury to file a lawsuit. For claims against government entities, the deadline is as short as 6 months. Missing this deadline can permanently bar your right to compensation - contact us today.

Evidence & Documentation Checklist

Gather as much of the following evidence as possible after your injury. Even partial documentation significantly strengthens your case.

1

Medical Records

  • Doctor’s reports detailing injuries & treatment
  • Hospital bills and receipts for all expenses
  • Prescription receipts for injury-related medication
  • Rehabilitation and therapy records
2

Accident Reports

  • Police or law enforcement reports
  • Incident reports from the property where injury occurred
  • Supervisor or manager reports (workplace injuries)
  • Any OSHA investigation or citation records
3

Witness Statements

  • Full name and contact information for all witnesses
  • Written or recorded statements taken at the scene
  • Contact details of first responders if applicable
4

Photographic Evidence

  • Photos of the accident scene before anything is changed
  • Close-up photos of the hazard or cause of injury
  • Photos of injuries at different stages of recovery
  • Photos of any property or vehicle damage
5

Insurance Information

  • Copies of your own insurance policies
  • Insurance information from the at-fault party
  • All correspondence with any insurance company
  • Any claim numbers or adjuster contact info
6

Employment Records

  • Records of all missed work days due to injury
  • Pay stubs or income statements showing lost wages
  • Employer verification letters if applicable
7

Legal Correspondence

  • Letters or emails from any previous attorneys
  • Copies of demand letters sent or received
  • Any settlement offers made by the other party
  • Court documents if a lawsuit has been filed
8

Miscellaneous

  • Vehicle or property repair estimates
  • Receipts for any out-of-pocket expenses incurred
  • Journal or diary documenting your pain and recovery
  • Any other documents related to the incident

What to Do Immediately After an Accident

1. Seek medical attention - Even if you feel fine, some injuries don't show symptoms for hours or days. A medical record created on the day of the incident is valuable evidence.

2. Document everything at the scene - Take photos, get witness names, and request copies of any official reports before leaving.

3. Do not admit fault or make statements - Anything you say to the other party or their insurance company can be used against you.

4. Contact LMS Law - The sooner you speak with an attorney, the better we can protect your rights and preserve critical evidence.

8 Common Mistakes That Hurt Your Case

Avoiding these common pitfalls can protect your claim and maximize the compensation you receive.

1

Delaying Medical Treatment

Seek medical attention immediately after any accident, even if you feel fine. Gaps in treatment give insurers grounds to argue your injuries weren't serious.

2

Failing to Document the Scene

Take photos and notes at the scene before anything is moved, repaired, or cleaned up. Visual evidence is often the strongest proof of negligence.

3

Not Preserving Evidence

Save damaged clothing, vehicles, or property - do not repair or discard them. Physical evidence can be critical to proving liability.

4

Not Tracking Expenses

Document every out-of-pocket cost related to your injury - no matter how small. Medical co-pays, parking fees, and transportation costs all add up and are recoverable.

5

Ignoring Your Attorney’s Advice

Follow your attorney’s guidance closely - it protects your claim at every stage. Decisions that seem minor can have major consequences for your case outcome.

6

Posting on Social Media

Anything you post publicly can be used against you by the defense. A photo of you at a party or a casual status update can undermine your injury claims.

7

Accepting Early Settlement Offers

Early offers from insurance companies are almost always far below what you actually deserve. Once you accept, you typically waive your right to seek additional compensation.

8

Waiting Too Long to Take Legal Action

California has strict statutes of limitations. Missing your deadline can permanently bar your right to compensation - contact LMS Law as soon as possible.
