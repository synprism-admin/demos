Prepare For Your Personal Injury Case

Home › Why LMS › Prepare For Your Case

When building your personal injury case, there are several types of evidence and documentation that can significantly strengthen your claim. Our attorneys have compiled this checklist to help you prepare.

Evidence & Documentation Checklist

🏥

Medical Records

  • Doctor's reports and diagnoses
  • Hospital bills and statements
  • Prescription receipts
  • Rehabilitation records
📋

Accident Reports

  • Police reports
  • Incident reports from the property
👥

Witness Statements

  • Contact information for all witnesses
  • Written or recorded statements
📷

Photographic Evidence

  • Photos of the accident scene
  • Photos of injuries at different stages of healing
🛡️

Insurance Information

  • Copies of all insurance policies
  • All correspondence with insurers
💼

Employment Records

  • Records of missed work days
  • Pay stubs or income statements
⚖️

Legal Correspondence

  • Letters from previous attorneys
  • Demand letters
  • Settlement offers
📁

Miscellaneous

  • Repair estimates
  • Any other relevant documents

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoiding these common pitfalls can protect your case and maximize your compensation.

1

Delaying Medical Treatment

Seek medical attention immediately after any accident, even if you feel fine.
2

Failing to Document the Accident Scene

Take photos and notes at the scene before anything is moved or cleaned up.
3

Neglecting to Preserve Evidence

Save damaged clothing, vehicles, or property — do not repair or discard them.
4

Not Keeping Track of Expenses

Document every out-of-pocket cost related to your injury, no matter how small.
5

Ignoring Legal Advice

Follow your attorney's guidance closely — it protects your claim at every stage.
6

Discussing the Case on Social Media

Anything you post publicly can be used against you. Stay off social media.
7

Accepting Early Settlement Offers

Early offers from insurers are almost always far below what you actually deserve.
8

Delaying Legal Action

California has strict statutes of limitations. Contact an attorney right away.
